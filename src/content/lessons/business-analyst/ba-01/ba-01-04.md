> **Prerequisites:** *The business analysis lifecycle.* You should know the stages a BA moves through, from understanding the need to evaluating value.

## Explanation

### What "competent" means for a BA

The BABOK Guide (the BA body of knowledge from the IIBA) lists **underlying competencies**: the abilities that make a BA effective whatever tools or methods the project uses. In practice they fall into six groups. Learn them as habits you practise, not as a list to memorise.

| Competency group | In plain English |
|---|---|
| **Analytical thinking and problem solving** | Breaking a messy situation into parts, finding causes, weighing options |
| **Behavioural characteristics** | Ethics, trustworthiness, adaptability, personal organisation |
| **Business knowledge** | Understanding the industry, the organisation and its solutions |
| **Communication skills** | Listening, questioning, writing, presenting, verbal and non-verbal communication |
| **Interaction skills** | Facilitation, leadership, teamwork, negotiation, conflict handling |
| **Tools and technology** | Office tools, modelling tools, collaboration and requirements tools |

### 1. Analytical thinking and problem solving

- **Define the problem first.** Write it as a sentence a stakeholder would agree with.
- **Ask "why?" repeatedly** to move from symptoms to causes. The "5 Whys" technique is a simple way to do this:

  | Why? | Answer |
  |---|---|
  | Why are deliveries late? | Orders are packed late. |
  | Why are orders packed late? | Pickers wait for stock lists. |
  | Why do they wait? | The list is printed once an hour. |
  | Why once an hour? | The old system exports in batches. |
  | Why is that a problem to fix now? | It is the real cause of most delays. |

- **Look for patterns and contradictions.** Two people describing the same process differently is a finding, not a nuisance.
- **Compare options** on cost, time, risk and benefit, and state your assumptions.

### 2. Behavioural characteristics

- **Ethics and integrity:** report what you find honestly, even when it is inconvenient, and respect confidential information.
- **Trustworthiness:** do what you said you would, and tell people early when plans change.
- **Adaptability:** switch between a senior executive and a call-centre agent in the same hour, and adjust when priorities change.
- **Organisation:** keep requirements, decisions and open questions in one place so nothing is lost.

### 3. Business knowledge

You cannot analyse what you do not understand. Learn:

- **How the organisation makes money and what it measures** (revenue, cost, customer satisfaction, risk).
- **Its terms and rules.** Every industry has its own words, such as "claim", "policy" and "premium" in insurance.
- **Its customers and competitors.**
- **How its systems fit together.**

Reading the company's annual report, shadowing staff and asking "What does this number mean?" are cheap ways to build business knowledge.

### 4. Communication skills

| Skill | How to practise it |
|---|---|
| **Listening** | Let the other person finish, then repeat back what you heard: "So the delay happens when..." |
| **Questioning** | Use open questions ("Tell me about...") to explore and closed questions ("Is it always...?") to confirm |
| **Writing** | Short sentences, one idea per requirement, no jargon the reader does not know |
| **Presenting** | Start with the conclusion, then the evidence, then the decision you need |
| **Visual communication** | A simple diagram often explains a process faster than a page of text |

**Open vs closed questions:**

| Open (explore) | Closed (confirm) |
|---|---|
| "How do you handle a refund today?" | "Does a manager always approve refunds over £500?" |
| "What problems does this cause?" | "Did this happen last week?" |

### 5. Interaction skills

- **Facilitation:** run a workshop with a clear goal, an agenda, ground rules, and quiet people drawn in.
- **Negotiation:** find what each side really needs. A position is "I want a report every day"; the interest behind it may be "I need to know about problems before noon".
- **Handling conflict:** separate people from problems, focus on the shared goal, and use facts.
- **Teamwork and leadership:** a BA often leads without authority, by being clear, reliable and helpful.

### 6. Tools and technology

You do not need every tool, but you should be comfortable with:

- **Office tools:** Excel for lists and quick data checks, Word or Confluence for documents, PowerPoint for presentations.
- **Modelling and diagramming:** draw.io, Visio or Lucidchart for process maps and UML.
- **Work management:** Jira and Confluence for backlogs, stories and documentation.
- **Prototyping:** simple wireframes in tools such as Figma or even on paper.
- **Basic data skills:** reading a table, simple SQL or Excel pivot tables to check a claim.

### Self-assessment: building a development plan

Rate yourself from 1 (new to this) to 5 (confident) on each group. Pick the **two lowest** and choose one action for each, for example "run a short workshop for a friend's project" or "learn to draw a swim-lane process map". Review in a month.

## Real-world examples

| Situation | Competency in action |
|---|---|
| Two departments each say the other causes the delay | **Analytical thinking:** map the process and measure the waiting time at each hand-off. The facts show where the delay really sits. |
| A director gives you five minutes at a lift | **Communication:** state the problem, the recommendation and the decision you need in three sentences. |
| A stakeholder demands a feature the team cannot build in time | **Negotiation:** explore the underlying interest and offer an option that meets it, such as a simpler version now and the full one later. |
| You are asked about a financial term you do not know | **Business knowledge and honesty:** ask for the meaning and an example, then add it to your glossary. |
| A workshop is dominated by one loud voice | **Facilitation:** use a round-robin or ask people to write ideas on notes first so everyone contributes. |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** You run a 45-minute workshop with five people to understand why customer complaints take too long to resolve.

**Step 1: Prepare.** Set the goal: *"Agree the main reasons complaints take long, and which one to tackle first."* Send an agenda, the goal, and a request to bring one recent example.

**Step 2: Open (5 min).** State the goal, ground rules ("one conversation at a time, no blame, parked topics go on a list") and the time limit.

**Step 3: Gather (15 min).** Ask each person to write two causes on separate notes, then read them aloud. Group similar notes on a wall or shared board. This hears the quiet people first.

**Step 4: Probe (10 min).** Use open questions on the largest group: *"Tell me about the last time this happened."* Confirm facts with closed questions: *"So the complaint waits in the shared mailbox until Monday?"*

**Step 5: Prioritise (10 min).** Each person gets three votes. The cause with the most votes becomes the first target.

**Step 6: Close (5 min).** Repeat the decision, list actions with owners and dates, and promise a written summary the same day.

**Step 7: Follow up.** Send the summary within a few hours, including the parked topics and open questions.

In one meeting you used **facilitation, questioning, listening, analysis and written communication**: five competencies working together.

## Practical example

A colleague tells you: *"The warehouse team is always late with the daily stock report, and it is making the sales team angry."*

1. Write **three open questions** you would ask the warehouse team.
2. Write **two closed questions** to confirm the facts.
3. Use the **5 Whys** on the statement "the report is late". Write four whys and answers you think are plausible, and say how you would check each.
4. Write the **one-sentence message** you would send the sales team while you investigate, so that they know you are on it without blaming anyone.

Then rate yourself on the six competency groups and pick one action for the lowest.

## Common mistakes

1. **Hiding behind tools.** Mastering Jira or Visio does not replace understanding people and problems.
2. **Asking only closed questions.** You will confirm what you already assume and miss what you did not know to ask.
3. **Listening to respond instead of to understand.** Summarise what you heard before you answer.
4. **Avoiding conflict.** Leaving a disagreement between stakeholders unresolved creates a bigger problem during delivery.
5. **Using jargon.** If the reader has to ask what a term means, rewrite it in plain words.
6. **Neglecting business knowledge.** A BA who does not understand the organisation will propose technically neat solutions that do not fit.
