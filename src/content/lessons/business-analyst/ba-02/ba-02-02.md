> **Prerequisites:** *What is a requirement?* You should know that a requirement states a need clearly and testably, without prescribing the solution.

## Explanation

### Requirements come in layers

Not every requirement is the same kind of thing. A CEO's goal ("grow online sales") and a developer's rule ("the Pay button is disabled until the form is valid") are both requirements, but they live at different levels and are owned by different people. The BABOK Guide groups them into four classifications. Using them stops you mixing up levels and helps you check that nothing has been left out.

| Type | Describes | Answers | Typical owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business requirements** | The goals and objectives of the organisation | *Why is the change needed?* | Sponsor, senior management |
| **Stakeholder requirements** | The needs of particular people or groups | *What do the people affected need?* | Users, customers, managers |
| **Solution requirements** | The capabilities and qualities of the solution | *What must the solution do and be like?* | BA with the delivery team |
| **Transition requirements** | What is needed to move from the old way to the new | *How do we get there safely?* | BA, operations, change team |

Solution requirements are split further into **functional** and **non-functional** requirements, which you will study in the next lesson.

### 1. Business requirements

These state **why** the organisation is investing, in terms of outcomes. They are usually measurable and relate to the strategy.

Examples:
- "Reduce the cost of handling a customer complaint from £12 to £8 within one year."
- "Increase online sales from 20% to 30% of total sales by December."
- "Comply with the new payments regulation by 1 March."

A business requirement does **not** mention screens, databases or features. If a statement does, it is probably a solution requirement.

### 2. Stakeholder requirements

These describe what a **specific person or group** needs in order to achieve the business goal. They bridge the gap between the organisation's goals and the solution.

Examples:
- "As a call-centre agent, I need to see a customer's last five orders on one screen so that I can answer questions without searching."
- "The finance manager needs a monthly report of refunds by reason to spot patterns."
- "Customers need to receive confirmation of their order immediately."

Stakeholder requirements are the source of many solution requirements. Different stakeholders sometimes want opposite things, which the BA must resolve using the business requirements as a guide.

### 3. Solution requirements

These describe what the **solution** must **do** (functional) and the **qualities** it must have (non-functional). They are detailed enough for the team to design and test.

Examples:
- *Functional:* "The system shall display a customer's last five orders when an agent opens a customer record."
- *Non-functional:* "The customer record shall open in under 2 seconds."

### 4. Transition requirements

These are needed **only during the move** to the new solution. After go-live they are no longer needed. They are often forgotten, and forgetting them causes painful launches.

Examples:
- "Data from the old system (customers and open orders from the last five years) shall be migrated to the new system."
- "All 120 agents shall complete two hours of training before go-live."
- "The old and new systems shall run in parallel for two weeks."
- "A rollback plan shall exist in case the launch fails."

### How the four types fit together

```
 Business requirement:    Reduce complaint handling cost from £12 to £8
            |
 Stakeholder requirement: Agents need all customer information on one screen
            |
 Solution requirement:    The system shall display customer details,
                          last five orders and open complaints on one page
            |
 Transition requirement:  Agents trained and customer data migrated before launch
```

Read from the top, the chain answers *why*, *for whom*, *what* and *how do we get there*. Read from the bottom, each item can be justified by the one above.

### How to use the classification in practice

- **Completeness check:** after drafting your requirements, ask "Do I have all four types?" Missing transition requirements is the most common gap.
- **Conflict resolution:** if two stakeholder requirements conflict, test each against the business requirements.
- **Communication:** executives mostly want to see business requirements, users want stakeholder requirements, developers need solution requirements, and operations teams need transition requirements. Present each audience the level they need.

## Real-world examples

| Project | Business | Stakeholder | Solution | Transition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **New online banking app** | Reduce branch visits by 25% in two years | Customers need to pay bills from their phone | The app shall allow a customer to pay a saved payee and show confirmation | Existing online banking users shall be invited and migrated in stages |
| **Hospital appointment reminders** | Reduce missed appointments from 15% to 8% | Patients need a reminder they can reply to | The system shall send an SMS 24 hours before each appointment and accept CANCEL | Patient phone numbers shall be checked and corrected before go-live; reception staff trained |
| **Warehouse barcode scanning** | Cut picking errors by 50% | Pickers need to confirm each item quickly | The handheld shall scan a barcode and show a green or red result within 1 second | Hand-held devices distributed; existing stock labelled with barcodes before launch |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** A travel agency wants to launch an online booking tool to replace phone and email bookings.

**Step 1: Business requirements.** After a meeting with the director:
- BR1: Increase the share of bookings made online from 10% to 40% within 18 months.
- BR2: Reduce the average cost of processing a booking from £25 to £15.

**Step 2: Stakeholder requirements.** Interviews with agents, customers and the finance team:
- SR1 (customer): I want to search holidays and book without calling.
- SR2 (agent): I want to see online bookings in my existing booking list.
- SR3 (finance): I need each online booking to produce an invoice automatically.

**Step 3: Solution requirements.** Derived from the stakeholder needs:
- SOL1: The system shall allow a customer to search by destination and date.
- SOL2: The system shall take card payment and send a confirmation email.
- SOL3: The system shall add each confirmed booking to the agents' booking list within one minute.
- SOL4: The system shall create an invoice record for every paid booking.
- SOL5 (non-functional): Payments shall comply with the card industry security standard.

**Step 4: Transition requirements.**
- TR1: Existing customer records shall be imported from the spreadsheet used today.
- TR2: Agents shall be trained on the online booking list before launch.
- TR3: For the first month, phone bookings shall continue alongside the online tool.

**Step 5: Check traceability.**

| Business | Stakeholder | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| BR1 | SR1 | SOL1, SOL2 |
| BR2 | SR2, SR3 | SOL3, SOL4 |

**Step 6: Look for gaps.** Is there a requirement that supports a business goal but is not covered? Is there a solution requirement that links to no goal? You find that SOL5 (security) has no stakeholder link. That is acceptable, because it comes from a regulation, so you add a note: *"Source: payment card regulations."*

## Practical example

A city council wants to "reduce the number of missed bin collections reported by residents."

Create at least **one requirement of each of the four types**:

1. A **business** requirement with a measurable target.
2. A **stakeholder** requirement from a resident, and one from a collection crew member.
3. A **solution** requirement (functional) and one non-functional.
4. A **transition** requirement.

*A possible business requirement:* "Reduce missed-collection reports from 600 to 300 per month within 12 months."

Check each against the descriptions above. If a statement mentions screens or technology and has no measurable outcome, it belongs further down the chain.

## Common mistakes

1. **Mixing levels.** Writing "increase profit" and "the button shall be blue" in the same list confuses readers and hides gaps.
2. **Skipping business requirements.** Without a goal, you cannot judge whether a stakeholder or solution requirement is worth building.
3. **Forgetting transition requirements.** The solution works, but nobody is trained, data is missing, and the launch fails.
4. **Treating every stakeholder wish as a must.** Stakeholder requirements need to be checked against the business goals and prioritised.
5. **Losing the links between layers.** If you cannot trace a solution requirement up to a goal, you may be gold-plating.
6. **Confusing the audience.** Showing executives a 100-line solution list or showing developers only a vision statement helps neither.
