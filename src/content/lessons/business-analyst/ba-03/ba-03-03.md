> **Prerequisites:** ba-03-02 Conducting stakeholder interviews

## Explanation

### Why workshops?

A **requirements workshop** brings key stakeholders together for a focused, facilitated session. Instead of ten separate interviews that produce ten different opinions, you get a shared understanding and decisions **in the room**.

| Interviews | Workshops |
|---|---|
| Depth from one person | Breadth and agreement across groups |
| Conflicts discovered later | Conflicts surfaced and resolved together |
| Many hours of BA time | Faster: one session can replace days of back-and-forth |
| Easy to schedule | Harder to get everyone in the room |

### Roles in a workshop

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Sponsor** | Opens the session, explains why it matters, may make final decisions |
| **Facilitator** (often the BA) | Runs the agenda, keeps discussion on track, stays neutral |
| **Scribe / note-taker** | Records decisions, actions, requirements and open questions |
| **Participants** | Subject matter experts and decision makers who contribute content |
| **Timekeeper** (optional) | Keeps each agenda item to time |

If you're facilitating, try to have someone else take notes. It's hard to do both well.

### Anatomy of a workshop

```
1. Opening      purpose, agenda, ground rules, introductions
2. Context      problem statement, scope, what's already known
3. Activities   brainstorming, process walkthrough, prioritisation
4. Decisions    agree outcomes, record open issues
5. Close        summary, actions with owners and dates, feedback
```

### Useful ground rules

- One conversation at a time
- Phones away except in breaks
- All ideas are welcome during brainstorming; judge later
- Stick to the agenda; off-topic items go to the **parking lot**
- Decisions are recorded on the wall/board as they're made

### Brainstorming techniques

| Technique | How it works | Why use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Classic brainstorm** | Everyone shouts out ideas; scribe writes all | Fast, energetic |
| **Silent brainstorm (brainwriting)** | Everyone writes ideas on sticky notes alone first, then shares | Stops loud voices dominating; more ideas |
| **Round robin** | Each person gives one idea in turn | Ensures everyone contributes |
| **Affinity mapping** | Group sticky notes into themes | Turns many ideas into structure |
| **Dot voting** | Each person gets 3-5 dots to vote on ideas | Quick, visible prioritisation |
| **Starfish / Start-Stop-Continue** | Sort ideas by what to start, stop, keep | Process improvement and retrospectives |

### Handling difficult dynamics

| Situation | Facilitator move |
|---|---|
| One person dominates | *"Thanks, Raj. Let's hear from someone who hasn't spoken yet."* Use silent brainstorming. |
| Someone stays silent | Ask them directly about their area of expertise; use round robin |
| Discussion goes off topic | *"Good point. Let's park it and come back if time allows."* |
| Conflict between participants | Bring it back to facts and the shared goal; capture both views; escalate to the sponsor if needed |
| Senior person's opinion shuts down debate | Collect anonymous ideas first; ask the senior person to speak last |

## Real-world examples

| Workshop | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Insurer: claims process | 12 people from claims, finance and IT mapped the as-is process on brown paper in 3 hours; found 4 duplicate checks |
| Retailer: loyalty programme | Silent brainstorm produced 60 ideas; dot voting picked 5 for the first release |
| Council: digital services | Start-stop-continue session with front-line staff identified 3 forms to scrap |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** A 2-hour workshop to agree pain points and priorities for the expense claims project.

**Before:**
- Objectives: agree the as-is process, top 5 pain points, and priority needs.
- Invite: finance manager, 2 finance clerks, 2 line managers, 1 employee representative, IT lead. Sponsor opens.
- Prepare: draft as-is process on large paper, sticky notes, dot stickers, agenda sent 3 days ahead.

**Agenda:**

| Time | Item | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Welcome, purpose, ground rules (sponsor) | |
| 0:10 | Walk through draft as-is process; correct it | Process walkthrough |
| 0:40 | What causes delays? | Silent brainstorm on stickies |
| 1:00 | Group into themes | Affinity mapping |
| 1:20 | Which problems matter most? | Dot voting (3 dots each) |
| 1:40 | Agree next steps and owners | |
| 1:55 | Close and feedback | |

**During:** The affinity map shows four themes: missing receipts, no mobile approval, unclear policy, manual data entry. Dot voting ranks missing receipts and mobile approval highest.

**After:** Within 24 hours, send photos of the boards, the corrected process map, the ranked pain points, decisions and actions.

## Practical example

You're facilitating and the IT lead says: *"This is all pointless; we'll just buy SAP Concur."* What do you do?

1. Acknowledge: *"That's a real option, thanks."*
2. Redirect: *"Before we compare tools, let's agree the problems any solution must solve, so we can judge options fairly."*
3. Park: write "Evaluate tools such as Concur" in the parking lot.
4. Follow up: include the tool in the options analysis later.

## Common mistakes

1. **No clear objective.** A workshop without a goal turns into a long meeting.
2. **Wrong people.** Without decision makers, nothing is agreed; without doers, the detail is wrong.
3. **Facilitator takes sides.** Stay neutral; your job is the process, not the answer.
4. **Too much content for the time.** Plan fewer items and finish them.
5. **No follow-up.** Decisions not written up and shared tend to be forgotten or disputed.
