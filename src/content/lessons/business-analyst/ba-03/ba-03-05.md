> **Prerequisites:** ba-03-04 Observation, surveys and document analysis

## Explanation

### Two checks before you build

Elicitation gives you raw information. Before anyone builds anything, you need to check it in two ways:

| | Confirming (verification) | Validating |
|---|---|---|
| **Question** | *Did I capture it correctly?* | *Is it the right thing to build?* |
| **Focus** | Accuracy and quality of what was written | Value: does it meet the business need? |
| **Who** | The stakeholder who gave the information, peer BAs, developers, testers | Sponsor, business owners, key users |
| **Example** | "Is it correct that managers approve claims over £500?" | "Will approving only claims over £500 reduce delays as we hoped?" |

A short way to remember it:
- **Verification**: building the requirements **right**.
- **Validation**: building the **right** requirements.

### Techniques for confirming

- **Send summaries** after every interview or workshop and ask for corrections by a date.
- **Walkthroughs**: read requirements aloud with stakeholders, step by step.
- **Peer review**: another BA, a developer and a tester review for clarity, consistency and testability.
- **Checklists**: run each requirement against the quality list (clear, atomic, testable, etc.).
- **Diagrams**: a process map or use case often exposes gaps faster than text.

### Techniques for validating

- **Trace to objectives**: does each requirement support a business objective?
- **Prototypes and wireframes**: let users try it and react.
- **Scenarios and examples**: walk through real cases end to end (*"A claim for £620 with a missing receipt..."*).
- **Acceptance criteria**: agree how you'll know each requirement is met.
- **Business case check**: does the set of requirements still deliver the expected benefit at an acceptable cost?

### Sign-off

**Sign-off** is formal agreement that requirements are complete and correct enough to proceed. In Waterfall it's usually a signed document. In Agile it's lighter: the Product Owner accepts stories into the backlog, and acceptance criteria are agreed before a sprint.

Sign-off doesn't freeze requirements forever. It creates a **baseline**: after it, changes go through **change control** so their impact is understood.

```
Elicit → Document → Confirm → Validate → Sign-off (baseline) → Change control
                       ↑__________________|
                       (rework if issues found)
```

## Real-world examples

| Situation | What confirmation / validation caught |
|---|---|
| Insurance portal | A walkthrough revealed "policyholder" and "customer" meant different people; the glossary was fixed before build |
| HR system | Prototype testing showed managers needed to approve leave in bulk, not one by one |
| Retail promotion engine | Scenario testing ("buy 2 get 1 free on mixed sizes") exposed a missing rule for cheapest item free |
| Government form | Tracing showed 6 fields didn't support any objective; they were removed, shortening the form by a third |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** You have drafted 15 requirements for mobile expense approval.

**Step 1: Send for confirmation.** Email each interviewee the requirements relevant to them with a deadline: *"Please confirm or correct by Friday."* Priya replies that approvals over £2,000 also need the finance manager. You add a requirement.

**Step 2: Peer review.** A developer flags that *"approve quickly from mobile"* isn't testable. You rewrite it: *"Managers shall approve or reject a claim in no more than 3 taps from the notification."* A tester asks what happens if the manager is on leave; you add a delegation requirement.

**Step 3: Validate with scenarios.** In a 1-hour session with the finance manager and two line managers, walk through three real claims:
- £45 taxi with receipt → approved on phone ✓
- £620 hotel with missing receipt → system blocks submission ✓
- £2,400 conference → manager approves, then finance manager ✓

**Step 4: Trace to objectives.** All 17 requirements support the objective *"reduce reimbursement time from 21 to 7 days"*, except one ("change the colour scheme"), which is moved to a separate backlog.

**Step 5: Sign-off.** The finance manager (business owner) signs off the baseline. Changes from now on are logged as change requests.

## Practical example

Classify each activity as **confirmation** or **validation**:

| Activity | Type |
|---|---|
| Sending an interview summary to the interviewee | Confirmation |
| Asking whether a feature will actually reduce call volumes | Validation |
| A tester checks every requirement is testable | Confirmation |
| Users try a clickable prototype of the new booking flow | Validation |
| Checking requirements against the glossary for consistent terms | Confirmation |
| Walking through a real customer case end to end with the business owner | Validation |

## Common mistakes

1. **Skipping confirmation because "I took good notes".** Your interpretation may still be wrong.
2. **Treating silence as agreement.** Set a clear deadline and follow up on non-responses.
3. **Validating only with managers.** End users spot practical problems managers miss.
4. **Seeing sign-off as a trophy.** It's a baseline, not a guarantee; changes will come.
5. **Huge documents for review.** Nobody reads 80 pages carefully; review in small, focused chunks.
