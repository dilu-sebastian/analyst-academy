> **Prerequisites:** ba-04-01 Why requirements documents exist

## Explanation

### The BRD in one sentence

A **Business Requirements Document (BRD)** describes **why** a change is needed and **what** the business needs from it, in language business stakeholders understand, so the sponsor can approve scope before detailed design begins.

### Standard BRD structure

| # | Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Document control** | Version, date, author, reviewers, change log, approvals |
| 2 | **Executive summary** | Half a page: problem, proposed change, key benefits |
| 3 | **Background and problem statement** | Current situation, pain points, evidence (numbers) |
| 4 | **Business objectives** | SMART goals the change must achieve |
| 5 | **Scope** | In scope, out of scope |
| 6 | **Stakeholders** | Who is affected, their role and interest |
| 7 | **Current state (as-is)** | Summary of today's process, often with a diagram |
| 8 | **Future state (to-be)** | How things should work afterwards, at a high level |
| 9 | **Business requirements** | Numbered, prioritised, high-level needs |
| 10 | **Business rules** | Policies the solution must follow |
| 11 | **Assumptions, constraints, dependencies** | What we take as true, limits, things we rely on |
| 12 | **Risks** | What could go wrong, impact, mitigation |
| 13 | **Success measures (KPIs)** | How we'll know it worked |
| 14 | **Glossary** | Business terms defined |
| 15 | **Sign-off** | Names, roles, dates |

### Writing the key sections well

**Problem statement:** use evidence.
> *Customers wait an average of 9 minutes on the phone to book appointments. 22% abandon the call. Reception spends 60% of its time on bookings.*

**Objectives: make them SMART.**
> *Reduce phone bookings by 40% within 6 months of launch.*

**Scope: be explicit about what's out.**
> *Out of scope: video consultations, payment collection, integration with the pharmacy system.*

**Business requirements: high level, numbered, prioritised.**

| ID | Requirement | Priority | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| BR-01 | Patients shall be able to book appointments online 24/7 | Must | Patient survey |
| BR-02 | Patients shall be able to cancel or reschedule online | Must | Reception workshop |
| BR-03 | Patients shall receive reminders before appointments | Should | Clinic managers |

**Business rules: separate from requirements.**
> *BRULE-01: Appointments cancelled with less than 24 hours' notice are recorded as missed.*

**Assumptions vs constraints vs dependencies:**

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Assumption | Most patients have an email address or mobile phone |
| Constraint | Must launch before the winter flu season (October) |
| Dependency | Requires the patient record system API upgrade, scheduled for July |

## Real-world examples

| Organisation | BRD headline objective | Notable out-of-scope item |
|---|---|---|
| Retail bank | Reduce mortgage approval time from 30 to 10 days | Changes to credit risk policy |
| Logistics firm | Cut failed deliveries by 25% | New vehicle fleet |
| University | Increase first-year retention to 90% | Changes to course content |

Notice how "out of scope" prevents expensive misunderstandings later.

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** Writing a mini-BRD for a gym chain's online class booking.

**1. Executive summary.** *Members cannot book classes outside reception hours, contributing to a rise in cancellations from 8% to 15%. This project introduces online booking to improve member retention and reduce reception workload.*

**2. Problem statement.** 15% quarterly cancellation; exit surveys cite "can't book classes" (41%); reception handles ~600 booking calls a week.

**3. Objectives.**
- Reduce cancellation rate to under 10% within 12 months.
- 70% of bookings made online within 6 months.
- Reduce booking calls by 50%.

**4. Scope.** In: class booking, cancellation, waitlists, instructor attendee lists. Out: personal training bookings, payments, mobile app (web only for release 1).

**5. Stakeholders.** Members, reception, instructors, gym managers, head office marketing, IT.

**6. Business requirements.**
- BR-01 (Must) Members shall book classes online up to 7 days ahead.
- BR-02 (Must) Members shall cancel bookings online.
- BR-03 (Should) Members shall join a waitlist for full classes.
- BR-04 (Must) Instructors shall see attendees before each class.

**7. Business rules.** BRULE-01: max 3 future bookings per member. BRULE-02: cancellations later than 2 hours before class count as no-shows; 3 no-shows in a month blocks bookings for 7 days.

**8. Assumptions / constraints.** Members have email; budget £30k; launch by January (New Year joiners).

**9. Risks.** Older members may struggle online → reception helps book during transition.

**10. Success measures.** Cancellation rate, % online bookings, call volume, member satisfaction score.

**11. Sign-off.** Operations director (sponsor), head of membership.

## Practical example

Spot the problems in this BRD extract:

> *Objective: Improve the customer experience. Scope: Everything related to orders. Requirement: Use Salesforce to manage orders quickly.*

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Objective is not measurable | "Reduce order-related complaints by 30% within 9 months" |
| Scope is unbounded | List specific processes in and out of scope |
| Requirement names a tool and uses "quickly" | "Agents shall view a customer's full order history on one screen" (tool choice later) |

## Common mistakes

1. **Too technical.** The BRD is for business readers; leave system detail to the FRD/SRS.
2. **No out-of-scope section.** Unstated exclusions become assumed inclusions.
3. **Vague objectives.** If you can't measure it, you can't prove success.
4. **Mixing rules and requirements.** Keep business rules separate and numbered so they can be reused.
5. **Writing it alone and presenting it at the end.** Build it incrementally with stakeholders.
