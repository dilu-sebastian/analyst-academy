> **Prerequisites:** ba-06-02 Events, activities and gateways

## Explanation

### Pools and lanes: who does what

| Element | Represents | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Pool** | A **participant**: usually a separate organisation or independent party with its own process | Customer, Online Shop, Payment Provider, Courier |
| **Lane** | A **role, department or system within** a pool | Sales, Warehouse, Finance (inside Online Shop) |

Key rule: **sequence flows (solid arrows) stay inside one pool.** They can cross lanes, but never pool boundaries. Between pools, participants communicate with **message flows** (dashed arrows with an open circle at the start).

```
┌ Customer ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│   ○ → [Place order] ──────────────────────────────── ◎✉ Receive parcel → ◉
└──────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────▲─────────────┘
           ┆ order (message flow)                         ┆ parcel
┌ Online Shop ────────────────────────────────────────────┆─────────────┐
│ Sales     │ ○✉ Order received → [Confirm order]          ┆             │
│ Warehouse │                       → [Pick & pack] → [Hand to courier]─┘│
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### White-box vs black-box pools

- **White-box (expanded) pool**: shows the internal process. Use for your organisation.
- **Black-box (collapsed) pool**: an empty pool with just a name. Use for external parties whose internal steps you don't know or care about (e.g. *Payment Provider*). Message flows connect to its edge.

### Message flows

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Connect two **different** pools | Shop → Payment Provider: "Payment request" |
| Can start/end at tasks, events or the pool edge | |
| Label with the message content | "Invoice", "Order confirmation" |
| Never connect elements in the same pool | Use sequence flow instead |

### Collaboration diagram

A diagram with two or more pools connected by message flows is called a **collaboration diagram**. It's ideal for showing how your organisation interacts with customers, suppliers and partners.

### Lanes vs pools: how to decide

Ask: **"Do these participants share one process controlled by one organisation?"**

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Sales, warehouse and finance in the same company | Lanes in one pool |
| Your company and a supplier | Separate pools |
| Your company and an outsourced call centre with its own process | Separate pools |
| A human role and the system they use | Lanes (or task types) in one pool |

### Data objects and stores

| Artefact | Notation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Data object** | Page with folded corner | "Invoice", "Order form" |
| **Data store** | Cylinder | "CRM database" |
| **Text annotation** | Open bracket with text | "SLA: 2 working days" |
| **Group** | Dashed rounded rectangle | Highlight a set of steps |

## Real-world examples

| Collaboration | Pools |
|---|---|
| Mortgage application | Applicant, Broker, Bank (lanes: Underwriting, Valuation, Legal), Valuer (black box) |
| Hospital referral | GP surgery, Hospital (lanes: Booking, Clinic), Patient |
| Online marketplace | Buyer, Marketplace, Seller, Payment Provider (black box), Courier (black box) |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** Model a restaurant's online delivery order as a collaboration diagram.

**Step 1: Identify participants.** Customer, Restaurant, Payment Provider, Delivery Partner.

**Step 2: Decide white vs black box.** Restaurant = white box (our process). Customer = white box (simple steps). Payment Provider and Delivery Partner = black box.

**Step 3: Lanes inside Restaurant.** Order System, Kitchen, Front of House.

**Step 4: Draw each pool's sequence flow.**
- Customer: ○ Hungry → [Place order] → ◎✉ Receive confirmation → ◎✉ Receive food → ◉
- Restaurant / Order System: ○✉ Order received → [Request payment] → ◎✉ Payment result → ◇X paid?
  - no → [Notify customer of failure] → ◉
  - yes → [Send confirmation] → Kitchen: [Prepare food] → Front of House: [Request courier] → [Hand food to courier] → ◉

**Step 5: Add message flows.**
- Customer [Place order] ┄► Restaurant start event: *order*
- Restaurant [Request payment] ┄► Payment Provider pool: *payment request*
- Payment Provider ┄► Restaurant intermediate event: *payment result*
- Restaurant [Send confirmation] ┄► Customer: *confirmation*
- Restaurant [Request courier] ┄► Delivery Partner: *pickup request*
- Delivery Partner ┄► Customer: *food*

**Step 6: Check rules.** No sequence flow crosses a pool; every message flow connects two pools and is labelled.

## Practical example

Find the errors:

1. A solid arrow from *Shop: Send invoice* to *Customer: Receive invoice*. → **Error:** sequence flow between pools. Use a message flow.
2. A dashed message arrow from *Sales lane* to *Warehouse lane* in the same pool. → **Error:** message flows are only between pools. Use a sequence flow.
3. A separate pool for *Finance department* of your own company. → **Usually wrong:** make it a lane in your company's pool, unless Finance is a genuinely independent participant.

## Common mistakes

1. **Pools for departments.** Departments of the same organisation are lanes.
2. **Sequence flows crossing pools.** The most common BPMN error.
3. **Modelling other organisations' internals** you don't know. Use black-box pools.
4. **Unlabelled message flows.** Name the message: "Invoice", "Payment confirmation".
5. **Too many lanes.** If a lane has one task, consider merging or using task annotations.
