## Explanation

### Today and tomorrow

Process improvement always involves two pictures:

| Model | Question | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **As-is (current state)** | How does the process work **today**, really? | Understand, measure, find problems |
| **To-be (future state)** | How **should** it work after the change? | Design, agree, define requirements |

The difference between them is the **gap**, and closing the gap is what the project delivers.

```
   AS-IS  ────── gap analysis ──────►  TO-BE
  (today)    what must change?       (future)
                    │
                    ▼
        Requirements, training, policy changes, systems
```

### Modelling the as-is

1. **Model reality, not the manual.** Use interviews, observation and data.
2. **Include workarounds** (spreadsheets, emails, phone calls).
3. **Add metrics**: volumes, times, error rates. Annotate tasks: *"avg 2 days wait"*, *"30% rework"*.
4. **Mark pain points** with numbered callouts or red flags.
5. **Validate** with the people who do the work.

### Analysing the as-is: typical waste

A useful lens from Lean is the **8 wastes**, remembered as **DOWNTIME**:

| Waste | Process example |
|---|---|
| **D**efects | Errors that need correcting |
| **O**verproduction | Reports nobody reads |
| **W**aiting | Approvals sitting in inboxes |
| **N**on-utilised talent | Experts doing data entry |
| **T**ransportation | Paper forms sent between offices |
| **I**nventory | Backlogs of unprocessed claims |
| **M**otion | Switching between 4 systems |
| **E**xtra processing | Approvals by three people for £20 |

### Designing the to-be

Common improvement patterns:

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **Eliminate** | Remove a duplicate approval |
| **Automate** | Service task checks budget instead of an email to finance |
| **Parallelise** | Run checks at the same time instead of in sequence |
| **Validate early** | Mandatory fields at submission to stop rework |
| **Self-service** | Customers update their own address |
| **Reduce handoffs** | One team owns the case end to end |
| **Set rules and SLAs** | Auto-approve under £100; escalate after 2 days |

### Gap analysis

| Area | As-is | To-be | Gap / change needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Submission | Email with scanned receipts | Online form with photo upload | New form; employee training |
| Approval | Manager by email, no reminders | In-app approval, reminder after 2 days, escalation after 4 | Workflow rules; mobile app |
| Budget check | Email to finance, 2 days | Automatic check against finance system | Integration with finance system |
| Payment | Weekly batch | Twice-weekly batch | Finance policy change |

Each gap becomes requirements, user stories or change activities.

## Real-world examples

| Organisation | As-is problem | To-be change | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Utility company | New connection requests passed between 6 teams; 45 days | Single case owner, parallel surveys | 18 days |
| Hospital pharmacy | Discharge prescriptions done after doctor rounds | Prescriptions prepared the evening before | Discharges 3 hours earlier |
| Retail returns | Refund only after warehouse inspection (10 days) | Instant refund for trusted customers under £50 | Satisfaction up 20 points |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** Expense claims take 21 days to reimburse.

**Step 1: As-is model (summary).**
Employee: [Fill paper form] → [Scan & email receipts] → Manager: [Review email] → ◇ Approve? → Employee: [Forward approval to finance] → Finance: [Re-key into finance system] → [Check budget with budget holder by email] → [Add to weekly payment run] → ◉

**Step 2: Annotate metrics and pain points.**
1. Manager review: avg 6 days (no reminders, can't approve on phone)
2. 25% returned for missing receipts (rework)
3. Re-keying: 10 minutes per claim, 3% typing errors
4. Budget check: avg 2 days
5. Weekly payment run: avg 3.5 days wait

**Step 3: Root causes.** Manual, email-based process; no validation; no integration.

**Step 4: To-be model.**
Employee: [Submit claim in app with photo] → System: ◇ Receipt attached? (no → prompt, cannot submit) → ◇X amount ≤ £50? → yes: [Auto-approve] / no: Manager: [Approve in app] (boundary timer 2 days → reminder; 4 days → escalate) → System: [Check budget automatically] → [Add to twice-weekly payment run] → ◉

**Step 5: Gap analysis and benefits.** Expected time: 21 → 6 days. Rework: 25% → under 5%. Re-keying: eliminated.

**Step 6: Validate to-be** with employees, managers and finance, then derive requirements from each gap.

## Practical example

Identify the waste and propose a to-be improvement:

| As-is observation | Waste | To-be idea |
|---|---|---|
| Customer details typed into CRM and billing separately | Extra processing / defects | Integrate systems; enter once |
| Orders wait overnight for a supervisor to release them | Waiting | Auto-release under a threshold |
| Paper files carried between buildings | Transportation | Digital documents |
| Senior engineers fill in timesheets for their team | Non-utilised talent | Self-service timesheets |

## Common mistakes

1. **Skipping the as-is** and jumping to the to-be. You can't measure improvement without a baseline.
2. **Modelling the ideal as the as-is.** Show reality, warts and all.
3. **To-be that's just the as-is plus software.** Automating a bad process gives you a fast bad process.
4. **No metrics.** Without numbers, you can't prioritise or prove benefits.
5. **Forgetting people and policy.** Many gaps are training, roles and rules, not systems.
