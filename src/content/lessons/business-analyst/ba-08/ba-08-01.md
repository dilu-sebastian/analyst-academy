## Explanation

### Two ways to deliver change

**Waterfall** delivers a project in sequential phases: each phase finishes before the next begins. **Agile** delivers in short cycles (iterations), producing working increments and adapting based on feedback.

```
WATERFALL
Requirements ──► Design ──► Build ──► Test ──► Deploy
(months)         (months)   (months)  (weeks)  (big bang)

AGILE
[Plan→Build→Test→Review] → [Plan→Build→Test→Review] → [Plan→Build→Test→Review] → ...
      Sprint 1 (2 weeks)         Sprint 2                  Sprint 3
      working increment          working increment         working increment
```

### Comparison

| Aspect | Waterfall | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements | Fixed and detailed up front | Evolve; detailed just in time |
| Delivery | One release at the end | Frequent small releases |
| Change | Expensive; formal change control | Expected and welcomed |
| Customer involvement | Mainly at start and end | Continuous |
| Documentation | Comprehensive (BRD, FRD, SRS) | Just enough (backlog, stories) |
| Risk | Discovered late (during testing) | Discovered early (each sprint) |
| Planning | Detailed plan up front | Rolling plan; adapt each sprint |
| Best for | Stable, well-understood, regulated, fixed-scope work | Uncertain, changing, innovative work |

### The Agile Manifesto (2001)

Four values. The items on the left are valued **more**, but the right still matters.

| We value... | ...over |
|---|---|
| **Individuals and interactions** | processes and tools |
| **Working software** | comprehensive documentation |
| **Customer collaboration** | contract negotiation |
| **Responding to change** | following a plan |

It also has **12 principles**, including: satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery; welcome changing requirements; deliver frequently; business people and developers work together daily; working software is the primary measure of progress; reflect regularly and adjust.

### Agile is a mindset; Scrum, Kanban are frameworks

| Framework | Key idea |
|---|---|
| **Scrum** | Fixed-length sprints, defined roles, events and artefacts (next lessons) |
| **Kanban** | Visualise work on a board, limit work in progress, continuous flow |
| **SAFe, LeSS** | Scaling Agile across many teams |
| **XP (Extreme Programming)** | Engineering practices: pair programming, TDD, continuous integration |

### Hybrid approaches

Many organisations mix the two: e.g. a Waterfall-style business case and high-level requirements, then Agile delivery in sprints. This is common in banks, government and large enterprises.

### How the BA role changes

| Waterfall BA | Agile BA |
|---|---|
| Writes large documents up front | Writes stories and AC continuously |
| Hands over requirements, then supports | Works with the team every day |
| Sign-off is a big event | Acceptance happens story by story |
| Change requests are formal | Changes go into the backlog and are re-prioritised |

## Real-world examples

| Project | Approach | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Building a bridge | Waterfall | Requirements fixed; changes mid-build are extremely costly |
| Regulatory reporting change with a fixed legal deadline | Waterfall or hybrid | Scope set by regulation |
| New mobile banking app | Agile | Customer needs uncertain; fast feedback needed |
| E-commerce site improvements | Agile (Kanban) | Continuous flow of small changes |
| ERP rollout in a large company | Hybrid | Fixed vendor contract, iterative configuration |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** A charity wants a new donation website. Should it use Waterfall or Agile?

**Step 1: Assess requirement stability.** The charity isn't sure which donation options donors want (monthly, one-off, gift aid, round-ups). → **Uncertain**.

**Step 2: Assess the need for early value.** A big fundraising campaign is in 3 months; they need something live soon. → **Early value matters**.

**Step 3: Assess customer availability.** The fundraising manager can join weekly reviews. → **Collaboration possible**.

**Step 4: Assess constraints.** Payment compliance (PCI) is fixed, but can be handled by a payment provider. → **Manageable**.

**Step 5: Recommend.** Agile: release a basic one-off donation page in sprint 2, then add monthly giving, gift aid and campaign pages based on donor data.

**Step 6: Explain trade-offs.** The final scope isn't fixed up front; instead, the charity controls priorities every sprint and gets value early.

## Practical example

Which approach fits better?

| Situation | Approach |
|---|---|
| Requirements are fully defined by a regulator and won't change | Waterfall (or hybrid) |
| A start-up testing whether customers want a product | Agile |
| A fixed-price contract with a detailed spec | Waterfall |
| An internal team constantly improving a live app | Agile (Scrum or Kanban) |

## Common mistakes

1. **"Agile means no documentation or planning."** It means just enough, continuously.
2. **"Waterfall is always bad."** It suits stable, well-understood, high-cost-of-change work.
3. **Calling it Agile but doing mini-waterfalls.** Agile needs real feedback and adaptation.
4. **Ignoring the mindset.** Stand-ups and sprints don't make a team agile without collaboration and learning.
5. **Assuming the BA disappears in Agile.** BAs are essential for understanding needs and writing good stories.
