## Explanation

### Scrum in a nutshell

**Scrum** is the most widely used Agile framework. It's defined in the **Scrum Guide** (Schwaber and Sutherland, latest 2020). A **Scrum Team** is small (typically 10 or fewer people) and has **three accountabilities**:

```
                ┌──────────── Scrum Team ────────────┐
                │                                    │
                │  Product Owner   Scrum Master      │
                │  (what & why)    (how we work)     │
                │                                    │
                │          Developers                │
                │      (how to build it)             │
                └────────────────────────────────────┘
                     ▲                      ▲
            Stakeholders / customers   Organisation
```

### Product Owner (PO)

**Accountable for maximising the value of the product.**

| Responsibilities | |
|---|---|
| Develops and communicates the **Product Goal** | |
| Creates and clearly communicates **Product Backlog items** | |
| **Orders** the Product Backlog | |
| Ensures the backlog is transparent, visible and understood | |
| Accepts or rejects work against acceptance criteria | |

The PO is **one person**, not a committee. Stakeholders influence the PO, but the PO decides the order of the backlog.

### Scrum Master (SM)

**Accountable for the Scrum Team's effectiveness.** A servant-leader and coach.

| Serves the team by | Serves the PO by | Serves the organisation by |
|---|---|---|
| Coaching self-management | Helping with backlog management techniques | Leading Scrum adoption |
| Removing impediments | Facilitating stakeholder collaboration | Removing barriers between stakeholders and teams |
| Ensuring events happen and are productive | Helping define the Product Goal | Coaching on empirical approaches |

The SM is **not a project manager** and doesn't assign tasks.

### Developers

**Accountable for creating a usable Increment each sprint.** "Developers" in Scrum means **everyone doing the work**: programmers, testers, designers, analysts.

| Responsibilities |
|---|
| Creating the Sprint Backlog (plan for the sprint) |
| Instilling quality by following the Definition of Done |
| Adapting their plan daily towards the Sprint Goal |
| Holding each other accountable as professionals |

Teams are **cross-functional** (have all skills needed) and **self-managing** (decide who does what and how).

### Where's the BA?

The Scrum Guide doesn't mention BAs. In practice, a BA usually:

| Option | Description |
|---|---|
| **Part of the Developers** | Most common. Works within the team on refinement, stories, AC, testing support |
| **Supports the PO** | Acts as the PO's "right hand", doing detailed analysis and stakeholder work |
| **Is the PO** | Some BAs grow into the PO role, especially for internal products |
| **Across teams** | In large organisations, a BA may support several teams on a shared domain |

### Common role confusion

| Myth | Reality |
|---|---|
| The SM is the team's boss | The SM coaches; the team self-manages |
| The PO tells developers how to build | The PO decides what and why; developers decide how |
| Testers aren't developers | Everyone doing the work is a Developer |
| Stakeholders can add work directly | Work goes through the PO |

## Real-world examples

| Organisation | Role setup |
|---|---|
| Bank digital team | PO from the business, SM, 5 developers, 1 tester, 1 BA within the Developers |
| Start-up | Founder acts as PO; a developer doubles as SM (not ideal but common) |
| Government service | Service owner, PO, delivery manager (SM-like), BA, user researcher, developers |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** A new team is forming to build a gym booking app. Assign roles and clarify the BA's place.

**Step 1: Product Owner.** The head of membership, Maria: knows members, owns the budget, can make decisions. She commits 50% of her time.

**Step 2: Scrum Master.** An experienced agile coach, Leo, part time across two teams.

**Step 3: Developers.** 3 software developers, 1 tester, 1 UX designer, 1 BA (you).

**Step 4: Clarify the BA's responsibilities.**
- Run discovery interviews with members and staff.
- Model the booking process.
- Draft stories and acceptance criteria for Maria to review and order.
- Facilitate refinement and Three Amigos sessions.
- Support testing and the sprint review.

**Step 5: Agree boundaries.** Maria decides order and accepts stories. You don't re-prioritise without her. Leo handles impediments, e.g. getting access to the gym's membership system.

## Practical example

Who is accountable?

| Situation | Role |
|---|---|
| Deciding the order of backlog items | Product Owner |
| Coaching the team to improve its stand-ups | Scrum Master |
| Deciding how to implement the booking API | Developers |
| Removing a blocker: the team can't get test data access | Scrum Master (helps remove the impediment) |
| Accepting a completed story | Product Owner |
| Writing acceptance criteria | PO is accountable; BA and team often write them together |

## Common mistakes

1. **PO by committee.** Many voices, no decisions; one PO must decide.
2. **SM as task-master.** Undermines self-management.
3. **Absent PO.** Without availability, stories stall and wrong things get built.
4. **BA and PO competing.** Agree clear responsibilities: PO decides, BA analyses and supports.
5. **Stakeholders bypassing the PO.** Leads to hidden work and confusion.
