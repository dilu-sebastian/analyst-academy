## Explanation

### Five events, one rhythm

Scrum work happens in a repeating cycle called the **Sprint**. Each sprint contains four other events. All events are **time-boxed** (they have a maximum duration).

```
┌─────────────────────────── Sprint (1-4 weeks) ───────────────────────────┐
│                                                                           │
│ Sprint      Daily Scrum   Daily Scrum   ...   Daily Scrum   Sprint    Sprint
│ Planning ──► (15 min) ──► (15 min) ──► ... ──► (15 min) ──► Review ──► Retro
│                                                                           │
│        (Backlog refinement happens continuously; it's an activity)        │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                   ▼
                            next Sprint starts immediately
```

| Event | Time-box (for a 1-month sprint; shorter for shorter sprints) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Sprint** | 1 month or less | Container for all work; produces a usable increment |
| **Sprint Planning** | Up to 8 hours | Decide why (Sprint Goal), what (items) and how (plan) |
| **Daily Scrum** | 15 minutes | Inspect progress towards the Sprint Goal; adapt the plan |
| **Sprint Review** | Up to 4 hours | Inspect the increment with stakeholders; adapt the backlog |
| **Sprint Retrospective** | Up to 3 hours | Inspect how the team worked; plan improvements |

For a 2-week sprint, teams typically use ~4 hours planning, 2 hours review and 1.5 hours retro.

### Sprint Planning

Three questions:
1. **Why** is this sprint valuable? → the **Sprint Goal**
2. **What** can be done? → selected Product Backlog items
3. **How** will the work get done? → the developers' plan (tasks)

> Sprint Goal example: *"Members can book and cancel classes online."*

### Daily Scrum

A 15-minute event for the Developers, same time and place every day. Focus: **progress towards the Sprint Goal** and impediments. The old three questions (*What did I do yesterday? What will I do today? Any blockers?*) are optional; many teams now "walk the board" instead.

It's **not** a status report to a manager. Detailed discussions happen afterwards.

### Sprint Review

A working session (not just a demo) where the team and stakeholders:
- See the increment working
- Discuss what was done and not done
- Discuss changes in the market or business
- Update the Product Backlog together

### Sprint Retrospective

The team reflects on **people, interactions, processes, tools and the Definition of Done**. Popular formats:

| Format | Questions |
|---|---|
| Start / Stop / Continue | What should we start, stop, keep doing? |
| Mad / Sad / Glad | Feelings about the sprint |
| 4Ls | Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed for |
| Sailboat | Wind (helps), anchors (slows), rocks (risks), island (goal) |

Output: 1-3 concrete improvement actions, often added to the next sprint.

### Backlog refinement

Not a formal event, but an ongoing activity (see ba-07-05).

### The BA in each event

| Event | BA contribution |
|---|---|
| **Planning** | Explains stories, answers questions, confirms AC, flags dependencies |
| **Daily Scrum** | Updates on analysis tasks; picks up questions from developers |
| **Refinement** | Brings ready stories, models, rules; facilitates splitting and AC |
| **Review** | Prepares scenarios for the demo; captures stakeholder feedback as backlog items |
| **Retro** | Shares observations on requirements quality and collaboration |

## Real-world examples

| Team | Practice |
|---|---|
| Insurance app team | 2-week sprints; review every other Thursday with claims handlers testing the increment live |
| Retail web team | Daily Scrum at 9:30 in front of a physical board; detailed discussions in a "parking lot" after |
| Bank team | Retro found stories arriving unready; introduced a Definition of Ready, cutting carry-over by half |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** A 2-week sprint for the gym booking app.

**Day 1: Sprint Planning (3.5 hours).**
- PO Maria proposes the goal: *Members can book and cancel classes online.*
- The team reviews the top 8 stories; BA explains rules (2-hour cancellation cut-off).
- Developers forecast 6 stories based on capacity; 2 stay in the backlog.
- Developers break stories into tasks.

**Days 2-9: Daily Scrums (15 min).**
- Day 4: Tester raises that the cancellation rules aren't clear for classes cancelled by the gym. BA clarifies with Maria after the Daily Scrum; a new story is added to the backlog (not this sprint).
- Day 6: Blocker: no test access to the membership system. Scrum Master escalates; resolved the same day.

**Day 10: Sprint Review (1.5 hours).**
- Team demonstrates booking and cancelling with real class data.
- Two reception staff try it; they ask for a "book on behalf of a member" feature.
- BA captures it as a new story; Maria places it high in the backlog.

**Day 10: Retrospective (1 hour).**
- Start/Stop/Continue: *Start* Three Amigos before each story; *Stop* starting stories without AC; *Continue* reviewing with real users.
- Action: BA schedules 30-minute Three Amigos sessions twice a week.

## Practical example

Match each situation to the event:

| Situation | Event |
|---|---|
| The team agrees "Members can join waitlists" as the goal | Sprint Planning |
| Stakeholders try the new feature and suggest changes | Sprint Review |
| A developer says they're blocked waiting for an API key | Daily Scrum |
| The team decides to pair on complex stories | Sprint Retrospective |
| The team splits an epic and estimates the stories | Backlog refinement |

## Common mistakes

1. **Daily Scrum as a status meeting** for a manager. It's for the Developers to plan their day.
2. **Review as a one-way demo.** Get stakeholders' feedback and update the backlog.
3. **Skipping retrospectives** when busy. That's when they matter most.
4. **Ignoring time-boxes.** Long meetings drain energy; time-boxes force focus.
5. **No Sprint Goal.** A list of unrelated items gives no focus or flexibility.
