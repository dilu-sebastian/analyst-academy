## Explanation

### Three artefacts, three commitments

Scrum has three **artefacts**. Each has a **commitment** that gives it focus and makes progress measurable.

| Artefact | What it is | Owner | Commitment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Product Backlog** | Ordered list of everything that might improve the product | Product Owner | **Product Goal** |
| **Sprint Backlog** | Sprint Goal + selected items + the plan to deliver them | Developers | **Sprint Goal** |
| **Increment** | Usable, done work added to the product | Scrum Team | **Definition of Done** |

```
Product Goal
    │
Product Backlog  ──Sprint Planning──►  Sprint Backlog  ──Sprint──►  Increment
(ordered, emergent)                    (goal + items + plan)        (meets DoD)
```

### Product Backlog

- The **single source** of work for the team.
- Contains user stories, bugs, technical work, spikes, improvements.
- **Ordered** by the PO (value, risk, dependencies, cost).
- **Emergent**: items are added, changed, removed all the time.
- Top items are small and detailed; lower items are larger and vaguer.

**Product Goal** example: *"Make class booking so easy that 80% of bookings happen online within 6 months."*

### Sprint Backlog

- Created in Sprint Planning; **owned by the Developers**.
- Contains the **Sprint Goal**, the selected backlog items, and the **plan** (often tasks).
- Updated daily as the team learns.
- If work turns out larger than expected, the team negotiates scope with the PO **without changing the Sprint Goal**.

### Increment

- A **usable** step towards the Product Goal.
- Must meet the **Definition of Done** to count.
- Several increments can be created in a sprint; they may be released at any time.
- Work that doesn't meet the DoD goes back to the Product Backlog.

### Definition of Done (DoD)

A shared, formal description of the quality required for an increment.

> **Example DoD**
> - Code reviewed by another developer
> - Unit and acceptance tests written and passing
> - Acceptance criteria met and accepted by PO
> - Deployed to the staging environment
> - Accessibility checks passed (WCAG 2.2 AA)
> - User help text updated

### Visualising the work: boards and charts

| Tool | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Task / Scrum board** | Columns like *To do → In progress → In review → Done* |
| **Sprint burndown chart** | Work remaining in the sprint, day by day |
| **Velocity chart** | Story points completed per sprint (for forecasting) |
| **Burnup chart** | Work completed towards a release, with scope line |


### Estimating with story points

**Story points** measure relative effort, complexity and uncertainty, not hours. Teams often use a Fibonacci-like scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) and **Planning Poker**, where everyone reveals an estimate at once and discusses differences.

**Velocity** = average points completed per sprint. Used for forecasting, never for comparing teams.

## Real-world examples

| Team | Artefact practice |
|---|---|
| Digital bank team | Product Goal printed on the board: "Open an account in under 5 minutes" |
| Retail team | DoD includes "performance test passed for checkout changes" after a Black Friday incident |
| Public sector team | Burnup chart shared with stakeholders to show scope growth against delivery |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** Setting up the artefacts for the gym booking product.

**Step 1: Product Goal.** Maria (PO) writes: *"80% of class bookings made online within 6 months, with fewer reception calls."*

**Step 2: Product Backlog.** BA and Maria create the initial backlog: 4 epics, 25 stories; top 10 refined with AC and estimates.

| Order | Item | Points |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Book a class | 5 |
| 2 | Cancel a booking | 3 |
| 3 | View my bookings | 2 |
| 4 | Join waitlist | 5 |
| 5 | Waitlist offer notification | 5 |
| ... | ... | ... |

**Step 3: Sprint Backlog.** In planning, with a velocity of ~15 points, the Developers select items 1-4 (15 points) and set the Sprint Goal *"Members can book, view and cancel classes, and join a waitlist."* They break items into tasks.

**Step 4: DoD.** Team agrees the DoD above.

**Step 5: Track.** Burndown updated daily. On day 6 the waitlist story looks bigger than estimated; the team talks to Maria, who agrees to move the waitlist email to the next sprint while keeping the Sprint Goal (join waitlist works; email comes later).

**Step 6: Increment.** At the review, 3 stories meet the DoD; the waitlist story meets AC but not "accessibility checks passed", so it's not done and returns to the backlog.

## Practical example

True or false?

| Statement | Answer |
|---|---|
| The Scrum Master owns the Sprint Backlog | False: the Developers do |
| An item that meets AC but fails the DoD is part of the increment | False |
| The Product Backlog can change at any time | True (the PO orders it) |
| Velocity should be used to compare teams' productivity | False |
| The Sprint Goal can change mid-sprint whenever the PO wants | False: if the goal becomes obsolete, the PO can cancel the sprint |

## Common mistakes

1. **Multiple backlogs for one team.** Keep a single Product Backlog.
2. **Undone work counted as done.** Only work meeting the DoD is part of the increment.
3. **Velocity as a target.** Pushing velocity up just inflates estimates.
4. **Weak DoD.** Quality debt accumulates and slows the team later.
5. **Stale backlog.** Hundreds of old items hide what matters; prune regularly.
