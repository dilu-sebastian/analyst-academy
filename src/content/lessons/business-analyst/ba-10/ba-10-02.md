> **Prerequisites:** ba-10-01 Identifying stakeholders

## Explanation

### Not every stakeholder needs the same attention

Once you have a list of stakeholders, you need to decide **how much effort** to spend on each and **what role** each plays. Two simple tools do most of the work: the **power-interest grid** and the **RACI matrix**.

### Power-interest grid (Mendelow's matrix)

Plot each stakeholder by **power** (ability to influence decisions or outcomes) and **interest** (how much they care or are affected).

```
            High │  KEEP SATISFIED        │  MANAGE CLOSELY
                 │  (high power,          │  (high power,
                 │   low interest)        │   high interest)
     POWER       │  e.g. CFO, IT director │  e.g. sponsor, PO
                 ├────────────────────────┼──────────────────────
                 │  MONITOR               │  KEEP INFORMED
                 │  (low power,           │  (low power,
                 │   low interest)        │   high interest)
            Low  │  e.g. other depts      │  e.g. end users, reception
                 └────────────────────────┴──────────────────────
                        Low           INTEREST           High
```

| Quadrant | Strategy | Typical actions |
|---|---|---|
| **Manage closely** | Engage fully; involve in decisions | Regular 1:1s, workshops, approvals |
| **Keep satisfied** | Meet their needs; don't overload them | Brief summaries, escalations, key decisions only |
| **Keep informed** | Communicate regularly; involve for detail | Newsletters, demos, focus groups, user testing |
| **Monitor** | Minimal effort; watch for changes | Occasional updates, general announcements |

**Watch for movement.** A "keep satisfied" CFO becomes "manage closely" when the budget is at risk. Low-power users gain power if they can refuse to adopt the system.

### Other analysis dimensions

| Dimension | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Attitude** (champion, supporter, neutral, critic, blocker) | Plan how to move critics towards neutral or support |
| **Impact** | High-impact groups need more change support |
| **Influence networks** | Who listens to whom? Informal influencers matter |

A **stakeholder engagement assessment** compares current vs desired attitude:

| Stakeholder | Unaware | Resistant | Neutral | Supportive | Leading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reception staff | | **C** | | D | |
| IT manager | | | **C** | D | |
| Sponsor | | | | **C** | D |

*(C = current, D = desired)*

### RACI matrix

**RACI** clarifies **who does what** for each activity or deliverable.

| Letter | Role | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **R**esponsible | Does the work | At least one per activity |
| **A**ccountable | Owns the outcome; signs off; "the buck stops here" | **Exactly one** per activity |
| **C**onsulted | Gives input before or during (two-way) | As needed |
| **I**nformed | Kept updated after (one-way) | As needed |

**Example: gym booking project**

| Activity | Sponsor | PO | BA | Dev team | IT manager | Reception |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Approve business case | **A** | C | C | I | C | I |
| Elicit requirements | I | A | **R** | C | C | C |
| Write user stories and AC | I | **A** | R | C | | C |
| Order the backlog | I | **A/R** | C | C | | |
| Technical design | | C | C | **A/R** | C | |
| UAT | I | **A** | R | C | | R |
| Go-live decision | **A** | R | C | C | C | I |

### RACI checks

- **Exactly one A per row.** Two As = confusion; zero = nobody owns it.
- **At least one R per row.**
- **Too many Cs** slow decisions.
- **A person with no blank cells** may be overloaded.
- Variations: **RASCI** adds **S**upport; **DACI** uses **D**river, **A**pprover, **C**ontributor, **I**nformed.

## Real-world examples

| Project | Insight from analysis |
|---|---|
| Bank app | The compliance officer had low interest but veto power → moved to "keep satisfied" with early reviews; avoided a late block |
| Factory system | Shift supervisors were informal influencers; winning them over drove adoption |
| Charity CRM | RACI showed two people thought they were accountable for data migration; resolved before it caused delays |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** Analyse stakeholders for the supermarket self-checkout project.

**Step 1: Rate power and interest (1-5).**

| Stakeholder | Power | Interest | Quadrant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operations director (sponsor) | 5 | 5 | Manage closely |
| Finance director | 5 | 2 | Keep satisfied |
| Store managers | 3 | 5 | Manage closely* |
| Cashiers | 2 | 5 | Keep informed |
| Shop workers' union | 4 | 4 | Manage closely |
| Licensing authority | 4 | 2 | Keep satisfied |
| Customers | 2 | 3 | Keep informed |
| Cleaning contractor | 1 | 1 | Monitor |

*Store managers are borderline; their support drives adoption, so treat them as manage closely.*

**Step 2: Assess attitude.** Cashiers and the union are worried about jobs (resistant). Desired: neutral or supportive.

**Step 3: Plan actions.**
- Union: early consultation, share plans for redeploying staff as self-checkout assistants.
- Cashiers: involve in design workshops; training; clear communication on roles.
- Finance director: monthly one-page summary on cost and benefits.
- Licensing authority: review age-verification process before pilot.

**Step 4: Build a RACI** for key activities (pilot store selection, age-check process, training, go-live).

## Practical example

Place each in the grid and suggest one action:

| Stakeholder | Quadrant | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Data protection officer (veto on personal data, low day-to-day interest) | Keep satisfied | Early review of data requirements; sign-off on DPIA |
| Customer service agents using the new system daily | Keep informed (high interest) | Demos, user testing, training plan |
| Product sponsor | Manage closely | Weekly 1:1; decision log |
| Marketing team for another product | Monitor | Include in general release announcements |

## Common mistakes

1. **Ignoring low-power, high-impact groups.** They decide whether the change is adopted.
2. **Static analysis.** Power and interest change; review regularly.
3. **Multiple As in RACI.** Shared accountability is no accountability.
4. **Sharing sensitive analysis carelessly.** Labelling someone a "blocker" in a public document damages trust.
5. **Analysis without action.** The grid is only useful if it drives engagement plans.
