## Explanation

### The right message, to the right people, in the right way, at the right time

Most project problems are communication problems: people surprised by decisions, flooded with irrelevant updates, or never told what changed. A **communication plan** sets out how you will keep each stakeholder group informed and engaged.

### What a communication plan answers

| Question | Example |
|---|---|
| **Who?** | Reception staff |
| **What?** | Changes to booking process, training dates, how their role changes |
| **Why?** | Reduce anxiety; prepare for go-live; gather feedback |
| **How (channel)?** | Team briefing + demo + one-page guide |
| **When / how often?** | Monthly briefing; weekly during the month before go-live |
| **Who sends it?** | BA with the store manager |
| **Feedback loop?** | Q&A at briefings; feedback form |

### Sample communication plan

| Audience | Purpose | Message / content | Channel | Frequency | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsor | Decisions, risks | Progress vs goals, risks, decisions needed | 1:1 meeting + 1-page report | Weekly | PM / BA |
| Steering group | Governance | Status, budget, milestones, escalations | Steering meeting + RAG report | Monthly | PM |
| Product Owner | Backlog decisions | Stories, questions, feedback | Daily conversation, refinement | Continuous | BA |
| End users | Awareness, input | What's coming, demos, how to give feedback | Demos, newsletter, user testing | Fortnightly | BA / PO |
| IT support | Readiness | Release dates, known issues, support guides | Email + handover session | Per release | Delivery lead |
| Wider organisation | Awareness | What's changing and why | Intranet article | At milestones | Comms team |

### Choosing channels

| Channel | Good for | Not good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Face-to-face / video call** | Complex, sensitive, persuasive conversations | Broadcasting to many |
| **Workshops** | Collaboration, decisions | Simple updates |
| **Demos / sprint reviews** | Showing progress, getting feedback | Detailed documentation |
| **Email** | Formal records, short updates | Debate, sensitive news |
| **Chat (Slack/Teams)** | Quick questions, team coordination | Decisions that need a record |
| **Confluence / intranet** | Reference information | Urgent messages |
| **Dashboards / reports** | Ongoing status | Explaining nuance |
| **Newsletters** | Broad awareness | Two-way discussion |

**Rule of thumb:** the more complex, sensitive or contentious the message, the **richer** the channel should be (face to face rather than email).

### Tailor the message to the audience

| Audience | Cares about | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Executives | Outcomes, cost, risk, decisions | 1 page, headline first, numbers |
| Managers | Impact on team, timelines, resources | Short summary + detail on request |
| End users | "What changes for me? When? How do I learn?" | Plain language, examples, demos |
| Developers | Precise requirements, rules, edge cases | Stories, AC, diagrams |

**Headline first (BLUF: Bottom Line Up Front):**

> ✗ *"Following our workshops in weeks 3 and 4 and analysis of data provided by the finance team, we considered several options..."*
>
> ✓ *"Recommendation: launch online booking in January with waitlists in March. Decision needed by Friday. Details below."*

### Status reporting

A common format for steering groups is **RAG** (Red / Amber / Green):

| Area | Status | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | 🟢 Green | On track |
| Schedule | 🟠 Amber | Integration testing 1 week late; recovery plan agreed |
| Budget | 🟢 Green | 45% spent, 50% complete |
| Risks | 🔴 Red | Payment vendor contract unsigned; go-live at risk; decision needed |

## Real-world examples

| Project | Communication practice | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital system rollout | Ward "champions" received early demos and briefed colleagues | Adoption faster than previous rollouts |
| Bank migration | Weekly one-page sponsor update with decisions needed | Decisions made in days instead of weeks |
| Retail change | Store staff learned about changes from a newspaper before internal comms | Trust damaged; plan rebuilt with managers briefed first |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** Create a communication plan for the gym booking go-live.

**Step 1: List audiences** from the stakeholder analysis: sponsor, PO, gym managers, reception, instructors, members, IT support, head office marketing.

**Step 2: Define what each needs to know.**
- Members: what's new, how to book online, when it starts, help available.
- Reception: how their role changes, training, how to help members.
- Instructors: how to view attendee lists.
- IT support: go-live date, known issues, escalation path.

**Step 3: Pick channels and timing.**

| Audience | 4 weeks before | 2 weeks before | Go-live week | After |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Members | Email teaser; posters | How-to video | Launch email; reception support | Survey at 4 weeks |
| Reception | Demo and Q&A | Hands-on training | Daily huddle | Feedback session |
| Instructors | Briefing at staff meeting | Quick guide | — | Feedback via survey |
| IT support | Handover plan | Support guide | Hypercare rota | Incident review |
| Sponsor | Weekly update | Go/no-go criteria | Daily update | Benefits report at 3 months |

**Step 4: Assign owners and feedback loops.** BA owns reception and instructor sessions; marketing owns member comms; delivery lead owns IT support.

**Step 5: Review and adjust.** After the first reception demo, staff ask for printed quick-reference cards; added to the plan.

## Practical example

Rewrite this update for a busy sponsor:

> *"Hi, just wanted to update you. We've had a lot of meetings this week and the team has been working hard. There are some issues with the payment provider; we've been talking to them a lot. Testing has started. Let me know if you have questions."*

**Better:**
> **Status: Amber.** Go-live 15 Jan is at risk due to payment provider delays.
> **Decision needed by Wed:** approve launching with card payments only (PayPal 4 weeks later), or move go-live to 29 Jan.
> **Progress:** booking and cancellation complete and in testing; waitlist on track for next sprint.

## Common mistakes

1. **One message for everyone.** Executives and end users need different content.
2. **Bad news by email.** Deliver difficult messages in person, then confirm in writing.
3. **Communicating only at milestones.** Silence breeds rumours.
4. **No feedback route.** Communication is two-way.
5. **Burying the headline.** Lead with the decision, risk or outcome.
