## Explanation

### Putting it all together

Case studies appear in BA training, assessment centres and interviews. You're given a scenario (a struggling business, a new product idea, a broken process) and asked to analyse it and recommend what to do. A case study tests whether you can **apply** the whole BA toolkit, not just define terms.

### A repeatable framework

Use the same structure every time. It mirrors the BA lifecycle you learned in ba-01-03.

```
 1. Understand   →  2. Stakeholders  →  3. As-is        →  4. Problems &
    the context       & goals            analysis           root causes
                                                                │
 8. Present      ←  7. Plan &        ←  6. Requirements ←  5. Options &
    & validate        measures            & to-be            recommendation
```

| Step | Key questions | Tools you've learned |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Understand the context** | What does the organisation do? What triggered this? What's the deadline? | Problem statement |
| **2. Stakeholders and goals** | Who's involved? What does success look like? | Stakeholder register, power-interest grid, SMART objectives |
| **3. As-is analysis** | How does it work today? Where are the pain points? | Process flows, BPMN, data |
| **4. Problems and root causes** | Why is it happening? | Five whys, fishbone, DOWNTIME wastes |
| **5. Options and recommendation** | What could we do? Which is best and why? | Options table, cost/benefit, risks |
| **6. Requirements and to-be** | What must the solution do? | BRD-style requirements, user stories, AC, to-be model |
| **7. Plan and measures** | How do we deliver and know it worked? | MoSCoW, MVP, KPIs, transition requirements |
| **8. Present and validate** | Does the audience agree? | BLUF summary, visuals, Q&A |

### Reading a case brief

Read twice. On the second pass, highlight:

| Highlight | Example from a brief |
|---|---|
| **Facts and numbers** | "Orders take 45 minutes to arrive on average" |
| **Stakeholders** | "Kitchen staff", "delivery partners", "the owner" |
| **Pain points** | "Customers complain about wrong orders" |
| **Constraints** | "Budget £20,000", "must launch before summer" |
| **Assumptions to test** | "The owner believes an app will solve everything" |
| **Missing information** | No data on order volumes by time of day |

### Make assumptions explicit

Briefs are deliberately incomplete. When information is missing:
- **State your assumption** clearly: *"I assume ~200 orders a day based on a mid-size restaurant."*
- **Explain how you'd verify it**: *"I'd confirm with the POS data."*
- Show how the recommendation changes if the assumption is wrong.

### Root cause tools: fishbone diagram

The **Ishikawa (fishbone) diagram** groups possible causes into categories.

```
  People        Process        Technology
     \             \               \
      \  untrained  \ no checklist  \ old POS
       \─────────────\───────────────\──────────► PROBLEM: Wrong orders (12%)
       /             /               /
      / unclear     / supplier      / noisy
     /  menu       /  substitutes  /  kitchen
  Policy        Materials      Environment
```

### Comparing options

| Criterion (weight) | Option A: Custom app | Option B: Third-party platform | Option C: Process fix + tablet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost (30%) | 1 | 4 | 5 |
| Time to value (25%) | 1 | 5 | 4 |
| Fit to need (30%) | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| Risk (15%) | 2 | 4 | 4 |
| **Weighted score** | **2.35** | **3.95** | **4.30** |

*(Scores 1-5, 5 = best. Weighted score = sum of score × weight.)*

### What assessors look for

- **Structure**: a clear, logical approach
- **Problem before solution**: you explore the need before recommending
- **Evidence**: you use the data in the brief
- **Stakeholder awareness**: you consider people, not just systems
- **Options**: you compare alternatives
- **Practicality**: MVP, phasing, risks, measures
- **Communication**: clear summary and visuals

## Real-world examples

| Case type | Typical brief | Strong answer includes |
|---|---|---|
| Process improvement | "Invoice processing takes 20 days" | As-is map, root causes, to-be, KPIs |
| New product | "A bank wants to launch a savings app" | Users, MVP scope, user stories, success metrics |
| System replacement | "Replace a 15-year-old CRM" | Stakeholders, as-is, requirements, options (buy vs build), transition plan |
| Problem diagnosis | "Customer complaints doubled" | Data analysis, five whys, prioritised fixes |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Mini brief:** *A dental practice has 3 dentists. Receptionists take bookings by phone 8am-6pm. 18% of appointments are missed. Patients complain about getting through on the phone. The owner wants "an app".*

1. **Context:** Small practice; revenue lost from missed appointments; poor phone access.
2. **Stakeholders and goals:** Owner (sponsor), dentists, receptionists, patients (incl. elderly), IT supplier. Goal: *missed appointments below 8% in 6 months; 50% fewer booking calls.*
3. **As-is:** Phone booking only; reminder calls made manually when receptionists have time (often not).
4. **Root causes (five whys):** Missed appointments → patients forget → reminders inconsistent → manual and low priority → no automated reminders.
5. **Options:** (A) custom app; (B) online booking + SMS reminders via existing practice software add-on; (C) hire another receptionist. **Recommend B**: low cost, fast, addresses both root causes.
6. **Requirements:** Patients book online 24/7 (Must); SMS reminder 48 h and 24 h before (Must); cancel via SMS reply (Should); waiting list for cancellations (Could).
7. **Plan and measures:** Pilot with one dentist for a month; KPIs: missed rate, booking calls, online booking share. Transition: train receptionists; keep phone booking for patients who prefer it.
8. **Present:** One slide BLUF: *"Recommend online booking and automated SMS reminders through our existing system; £X/month; expected to halve missed appointments."*

## Practical example

Before answering any case, write these headings on a page and fill one line each in 5 minutes:

1. The real problem is...
2. Success looks like...
3. Key stakeholders are...
4. The biggest pain point is...
5. My top assumption is...
6. My likely recommendation is... (to be tested)

This gives you a skeleton to develop.

## Common mistakes

1. **Jumping to a solution.** Assessors look for problem-first thinking.
2. **Ignoring data in the brief.** Numbers are there to be used.
3. **Only one option.** Show you compared alternatives.
4. **Forgetting people and change.** Training, communication and adoption matter.
5. **No measures of success.** Always define KPIs.
6. **Poor time management.** Allocate time to each step; leave time to summarise.
