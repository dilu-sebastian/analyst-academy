## Explanation

### The brief

> **Spice Route** is a family-owned Indian restaurant group with **3 restaurants** in one city. It takes delivery orders by **phone** and through **two third-party delivery apps**, which charge **30% commission**.
>
> - ~**250 delivery orders a day** across all sites; 60% via apps, 40% by phone.
> - App commission costs about **£22,000 a month**.
> - Phone lines are busy 6-8 pm; staff estimate **15-20% of callers give up**.
> - **9% of orders** have an error (wrong item, missing item, wrong address).
> - Kitchen staff write phone orders on paper; app orders arrive on separate tablets (one per app).
> - Average delivery time: **52 minutes**; competitors advertise 35-40.
> - The owner, Ravi, wants *"our own ordering app like the big chains"*. Budget: **£60,000** for year one. He'd like it live before the **Diwali** season in 5 months.

This lesson walks through the whole case using the 8-step framework. Each section below is what you would produce.

### 1. Understand the context

**Problem statement:**
> *Spice Route loses margin to high app commissions and loses orders to busy phone lines, while order errors and slow deliveries harm customer satisfaction.*

**Ravi's request ("our own app") is a solution.** The underlying needs are: lower cost per order, capture lost phone orders, fewer errors, faster delivery.

### 2. Stakeholders and goals

| Stakeholder | Interest | Power | Interest level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ravi (owner, sponsor) | Profit, brand, growth | High | High |
| Restaurant managers (3) | Smooth service, staff workload | Medium | High |
| Kitchen staff | Clear orders, manageable peaks | Low | High |
| Front-of-house / phone staff | Fewer calls, less stress | Low | High |
| Delivery drivers (own + app couriers) | Clear addresses, ready food | Low | Medium |
| Customers | Easy ordering, accuracy, speed, value | Medium | High |
| Delivery apps | Keep volume and commission | Medium | Medium |
| Accountant | Costs, payment reconciliation | Medium | Low |

**SMART objectives (12 months):**
1. Move **30% of third-party app orders** to direct ordering (own web ordering), avoiding ~£6,600/month in commission (before the small direct-ordering fee).
2. Cut order errors from **9% to under 3%**.
3. Reduce average delivery time from **52 to 40 minutes**.
4. Reduce abandoned phone calls at peak to **under 5%**.

### 3. As-is analysis

```
Customer   | ○ Hungry → [Call restaurant] → ◇ Line busy? ─yes→ [Give up / use app] → ◉ Lost order
           |                                 │no
Phone staff|                          [Take order on paper] → [Read back?] (often skipped)
Kitchen    |                          [Read paper ticket] → [Cook] → [Pack]
           |  App orders: [Tablet A beeps] / [Tablet B beeps] → [Re-write on paper] → [Cook]
Driver     |                          [Read handwritten address] → [Deliver] → ◉
```

**Pain points (with waste type):**

| # | Pain point | Evidence | Waste |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Busy phone lines at peak | 15-20% callers give up | Waiting |
| P2 | Paper tickets and re-writing app orders | 9% errors | Defects, extra processing |
| P3 | 3 order channels with separate tablets | Staff switch between devices | Motion |
| P4 | 30% commission on 60% of orders | £22k/month | Cost (not a Lean waste, but key) |
| P5 | No order-ready signal to drivers | Drivers wait or leave early | Waiting |
| P6 | Handwritten addresses | Wrong-address deliveries | Defects |

### 4. Root causes

**Five whys on errors:** Errors 9% → orders transcribed by hand → paper tickets are the only kitchen system → no central order system → each channel added ad hoc.

**Root causes:** (1) no single order management system, (2) phone as the only direct channel, (3) no structured kitchen workflow.

### 5. Options and recommendation

| Option | Description | Year-1 cost (estimate) | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Custom native app** (iOS + Android) | Build own branded app with ordering and loyalty | £80-120k | 6-9 months |
| **B. White-label online ordering + order aggregator** | Branded ordering website / web app from a restaurant-ordering provider; one kitchen display combining direct and third-party app orders | £15-25k + ~3-5% per order | 6-10 weeks |
| **C. Improve phone handling only** | Extra phone staff at peak; order-taking script; read-back | ~£25k staff cost | Immediate |
| **D. Do nothing** | — | £0 | — |

**Weighted scoring (1-5, 5 = best):**

| Criterion | Weight | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cost within budget | 25% | 1 | 5 | 4 | 5 |
| Time to value (before Diwali) | 20% | 1 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Reduces commission | 20% | 5 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| Reduces errors | 20% | 4 | 5 | 2 | 1 |
| Risk | 15% | 2 | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| **Weighted total** | | **2.55** | **4.65** | **3.4** | **2.95** |

**Recommendation: Option B**, with a later review of a native app if direct orders exceed targets. Combine with low-cost elements of C (order read-back script) immediately.

> *Rationale:* fits budget and Diwali deadline, tackles all three root causes, and lowers commission. A custom app costs more than the budget, can't be live in time, and customers already have food apps on their phones; a branded web ordering link promoted via receipts, flyers and Google is enough to start.

### 6. Requirements and to-be

**To-be process:**
```
Customer   | ○ Hungry → [Order on Spice Route website] ─or─ [Call] ─or─ [Third-party app]
System     |   → [All orders into one order system] → [Send confirmation + ETA]
Kitchen    |   → [Order appears on kitchen display, by prep time] → [Cook] → [Mark ready]
Driver     |   → [Notified: order ready; address on driver app] → [Deliver] → [Mark delivered] → ◉
```

**Key business requirements (MoSCoW):**

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| BR-01 | Customers can order delivery or collection online from any of the 3 restaurants | Must |
| BR-02 | Customers can pay online by card | Must |
| BR-03 | All orders (web, phone, apps) appear on one kitchen display per restaurant | Must |
| BR-04 | Phone orders are entered directly into the system, not on paper | Must |
| BR-05 | Customers receive order confirmation and an estimated time | Must |
| BR-06 | Addresses are validated using postcode lookup | Should |
| BR-07 | Drivers are notified when an order is ready | Should |
| BR-08 | Customers can reorder a previous order | Could |
| BR-09 | Loyalty points | Won't (this year) |

**Sample user stories and AC:**

> **Story:** As a returning customer, I want to order my usual meal online so that I don't have to wait on the phone.
> **AC:**
> 1. Customer can choose a restaurant by postcode (delivery radius 5 miles).
> 2. Menu shows only items available at that restaurant now.
> 3. Payment by card; failed payment keeps the basket.
> 4. Confirmation email/SMS within 2 minutes, with estimated delivery time.

> **Story:** As a kitchen manager, I want all orders on one screen ordered by required start time so that we cook in the right sequence.
> **Gherkin:**
> ```gherkin
> Scenario: App and web orders appear together
>   Given a web order placed at 18:02 and an app order placed at 18:03
>   When both are accepted
>   Then both appear on the kitchen display in placement order
>   And each shows its channel and any allergy notes
> ```

**NFRs:** order page loads < 3 s on mobile; 99.5% availability 11:00-23:00; PCI-compliant payments via provider; allergy notes always visible to kitchen.

**Transition requirements:** train staff at each site; run paper backup for 2 weeks; update Google Business profiles and print flyers with the ordering link; migrate menu and prices.

### 7. Plan and measures

| Phase | Scope | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Quick wins** | Phone read-back script; address confirmation | Week 1 |
| **MVP pilot** | Web ordering + kitchen display at **1 restaurant** | Weeks 3-8 |
| **Rollout** | All 3 restaurants; app order aggregation; driver notifications | Weeks 9-14 |
| **Diwali campaign** | Promote direct ordering (e.g. 10% off first web order, still cheaper than commission) | Month 5 |
| **Review** | Assess native app and loyalty | Month 9 |

**KPIs:** direct order share, commission £/month, error rate, average delivery time, abandoned calls at peak, customer rating.

**Risks:** low customer uptake (mitigate with incentives and promotion); staff resistance (involve managers early, train); provider outage (paper fallback).

### 8. Present and validate

**One-slide summary for Ravi (BLUF):**
> **Recommendation:** launch branded online ordering with a single kitchen order system across all 3 restaurants within 14 weeks, ready for Diwali. **Year-1 cost** ~£25k, within budget. **Expected benefits:** ~£6.6k/month lower commission, errors down from 9% to <3%, faster deliveries. A custom app can be reconsidered once direct ordering is proven.

Then walk through the as-is pain points, options comparison and pilot plan, and ask for a decision on the pilot restaurant.

## Real-world examples

Many independent restaurants use white-label ordering platforms to reduce reliance on high-commission marketplaces, while keeping marketplaces for discovery. The pattern in this case (direct channel + aggregation into one kitchen screen + promotion) is common in the industry.

## Practical example

Ravi pushes back: *"I still want my own app like the big chains."* How do you respond?

1. Acknowledge the goal behind it: brand presence and customer loyalty.
2. Show the evidence: cost (£80-120k vs £60k budget), timeline (miss Diwali), and that the web app meets the same needs.
3. Offer a path: *"Let's prove demand with web ordering. If direct orders reach 30% by month 9, we'll have data to justify a native app, possibly with loyalty."*
4. Record the decision and the review point.

## Common mistakes

1. **Accepting "build an app" without analysis.** The brief invites you to challenge it.
2. **Ignoring the third-party apps.** They still bring discovery and volume; aggregation is key.
3. **No kitchen focus.** Errors and delays happen in the kitchen workflow, not just ordering.
4. **No transition plan.** Training, fallback and promotion decide success.
5. **No numbers.** Use the brief's data in objectives, scoring and benefits.
