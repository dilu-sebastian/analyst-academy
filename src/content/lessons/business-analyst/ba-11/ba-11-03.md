## Explanation

### Analysis only matters if people act on it

You can do brilliant analysis and still fail if the decision-makers don't understand it, trust it or act on it. Presenting recommendations is a core BA skill, used in steering meetings, sponsor briefings, assessment centres and interviews.

### Structure: the Pyramid Principle

Barbara Minto's **Pyramid Principle**: start with the answer, then support it with grouped arguments, then evidence.

```
                 ┌──────────────────────────────┐
                 │  RECOMMENDATION (the answer) │
                 └──────────────┬───────────────┘
        ┌───────────────────────┼───────────────────────┐
  ┌─────┴─────┐           ┌─────┴─────┐           ┌─────┴─────┐
  │ Reason 1  │           │ Reason 2  │           │ Reason 3  │
  └─────┬─────┘           └─────┬─────┘           └─────┬─────┘
    evidence                evidence                evidence
```

Executives often stop listening after the first minute, so put the **decision** there.

### A proven presentation flow

| Section | Content | Time (10-min slot) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Headline (BLUF)** | Recommendation + decision needed | 1 min |
| **2. The problem** | What's wrong, with 2-3 key numbers | 1.5 min |
| **3. What we found** | Root causes (one visual: process map or fishbone) | 2 min |
| **4. Options** | 3-4 options compared (one table) | 2 min |
| **5. Recommendation** | Why this option, benefits, costs | 1.5 min |
| **6. Plan, risks, measures** | Phases, top 3 risks, KPIs | 1 min |
| **7. Ask** | The decision or support you need | 1 min |

### SCQA: a storytelling opener

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **S**ituation | Spice Route takes 250 delivery orders a day across 3 restaurants. |
| **C**omplication | 60% come via apps charging 30% commission, 9% of orders have errors, and peak callers give up. |
| **Q**uestion | How can we cut costs and errors before Diwali within £60k? |
| **A**nswer | Launch branded online ordering with one kitchen system across all sites in 14 weeks. |

### Slide design principles

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| One message per slide, written as the **title** ("Errors fall from 9% to under 3% with one order system") | Generic titles ("Analysis") |
| Visuals: process maps, simple charts, tables | Walls of text |
| Big numbers for key facts | 8-point font tables |
| 5-8 slides for a 10-minute slot | 25 slides |
| Appendix for detail | Everything in the main deck |

### Tailor to the audience

| Audience | Focus |
|---|---|
| Sponsor / executives | Decision, cost, benefits, risk, timing |
| Operational managers | Impact on teams, process changes, training |
| Technical teams | Scope, integrations, NFRs, constraints |
| Users | What changes for them, when, support available |

### Handling questions and objections

1. **Listen fully**; don't interrupt.
2. **Clarify**: *"Do you mean the cost in year one or ongoing?"*
3. **Answer briefly** with evidence.
4. **If you don't know**: *"I don't have that figure; I'll confirm by tomorrow."* Then do it.
5. **For objections**, acknowledge and explore the concern: *"That's a fair concern about staff adoption. Here's how the pilot addresses it..."*
6. **Bridge back** to the recommendation.

### Common objections and responses

| Objection | Response approach |
|---|---|
| "It's too expensive." | Compare with the cost of doing nothing; show payback period |
| "We tried this before." | Ask what happened; show what's different now |
| "Staff won't use it." | Show involvement, training, pilot, champions |
| "Why not option X?" | Refer to the scoring; explain trade-offs |
| "Can we do it faster?" | Show what could be cut (MoSCoW) and the risk |

### Getting a decision

End with a **clear ask**:
> *"We're asking for approval to start the pilot at the Northside restaurant on 3 June, with £25k for year one. Can we agree that today?"*

Then confirm decisions and actions in writing afterwards.

## Real-world examples

| Situation | What made the difference |
|---|---|
| Bank steering committee | BA led with "Decision needed: approve phase 2 (£300k) or pause". Decision made in 10 minutes |
| Hospital board | One slide showing patient wait time before/after the pilot won support more than the 40-page report |
| Assessment centre | Candidates who stated a recommendation in the first minute scored higher than those who built up to it |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Scenario:** Present the Spice Route recommendation to Ravi and his three managers (10 minutes).

**Slide 1: Headline.**
> *"Recommendation: branded online ordering with one kitchen order system, live in 14 weeks for Diwali. Year-1 cost ~£25k. Decision today: approve a pilot at Northside."*

**Slide 2: The problem (3 big numbers).**
> £22k/month in app commission · 9% of orders have errors · 15-20% of peak callers give up

**Slide 3: What we found.** Simplified as-is process map with 3 red pain points: paper tickets, separate tablets, busy phone lines.

**Slide 4: Options.** Weighted scoring table: custom app 2.55, white-label + aggregation 4.65, more phone staff 3.4, do nothing 2.95.

**Slide 5: Why this option.** Within budget, live before Diwali, fixes the root causes, keeps third-party apps for discovery.

**Slide 6: Plan, risks, KPIs.** Timeline (quick wins → pilot → rollout → Diwali campaign → review); top risks; KPIs.

**Slide 7: The ask.** Approve pilot at Northside starting 3 June; nominate the Northside manager as champion.

**Q&A:** Ravi asks, *"Why not my own app?"* → *"It's a great long-term goal. It would cost £80-120k and miss Diwali. Web ordering proves demand first; if direct orders hit 30%, we'll have data to justify the app."*

**After:** Send a summary email: decisions, actions, owners, dates.

## Practical example

Rewrite this weak opening:

> *"Hi everyone, thanks for coming. So, over the last few weeks we've been doing lots of interviews and workshops and looking at processes and data, and there are quite a few interesting findings, which I'll go through..."*

**Better:**
> *"I'm recommending we launch branded online ordering across all three restaurants before Diwali. It cuts commission by around £6,600 a month and should reduce order errors from 9% to under 3%. Today I need your approval to start a pilot at Northside. Let me show you why."*

## Common mistakes

1. **Building up to the answer.** Lead with it.
2. **Too much detail.** Move it to the appendix.
3. **No clear ask.** Meetings end without decisions.
4. **Defensive responses to objections.** Treat them as useful information.
5. **No follow-up.** Confirm decisions and actions in writing.
