## Explanation

### "What would you do if...?"

**Scenario (situational) questions** describe a hypothetical situation and ask how you'd handle it. They test **judgement**, **structure** and whether you apply BA principles under pressure. There's rarely a single right answer; interviewers look for a sensible, stakeholder-aware **approach**.

### A structure for scenario answers: CLARIFY-ANALYSE-ACT-CONFIRM

```
 1. CLARIFY   What would I want to understand first? (questions, context)
 2. ANALYSE   What's really going on? (root cause, stakeholders, impact)
 3. ACT       What would I do? (steps, techniques, options, who I involve)
 4. CONFIRM   How would I check it worked / close the loop? (validation, documentation, measures)
```

Optionally finish with: *"For example, when I..."* to link to real experience.

### Principles interviewers want to hear

| Principle | Phrase it as |
|---|---|
| Problem before solution | "First I'd want to understand the underlying need..." |
| Talk to the right people | "I'd speak to X and Y separately, then together..." |
| Use evidence | "I'd look at the data / process / logs..." |
| Make trade-offs visible | "I'd show the impact on scope, time and cost..." |
| Respect roles | "The PO/sponsor decides; I'd bring options and a recommendation." |
| Document decisions | "I'd record the decision and update the backlog / requirements." |
| Stay calm and neutral | "I wouldn't take sides; I'd focus on the shared objective." |

### Common scenario questions and model answers

**1. "A stakeholder keeps changing requirements."**
> **Clarify:** Why are they changing: new information, unclear goals, or pressure from others? **Analyse:** Check whether the original requirement was ambiguous or the business context has changed. **Act:** Walk through the impact of each change on scope, time and cost; in Agile, put changes in the backlog for the PO to prioritise; in Waterfall, use change control. Use prototypes or examples early so they can see what they're getting. **Confirm:** Document decisions, get agreement on a baseline, and review in regular check-ins.

**2. "Developers say a requirement can't be built."**
> **Clarify:** Is it technically impossible, too expensive, or too slow to build? **Analyse:** Revisit the underlying need with the stakeholder. **Act:** Work with developers and the architect on alternatives that meet the need; present options and trade-offs to the PO/sponsor. **Confirm:** Update the requirement and acceptance criteria; record the decision.

**3. "Two senior stakeholders want opposite things."**
> **Clarify:** Meet each separately to understand positions and interests. **Analyse:** Link both to business objectives and gather data. **Act:** Facilitate a joint session to generate options; evaluate against objective criteria. If no agreement, escalate to the sponsor with options and a recommendation. **Confirm:** Document the decision and communicate it to both.

**4. "You join a project halfway and there's no documentation."**
> **Clarify:** Meet the PM/PO for goals, scope and current status. **Analyse:** Review Jira, emails and any notes; interview key team members and stakeholders; map the current understanding. **Act:** Create lightweight documentation: scope summary, process map, backlog clean-up, open questions. **Confirm:** Validate with stakeholders and agree how documentation will be maintained.

**5. "UAT finds the solution doesn't meet the business need, but it meets the documented requirements."**
> **Clarify:** What exactly doesn't meet the need? Was it misunderstood, missed, or has the need changed? **Analyse:** Trace back to the requirements and sign-off. **Act:** Raise as a change request or new backlog item with impact; prioritise with the business owner; decide whether it blocks go-live or can follow. **Confirm:** Run a lessons-learned review: earlier validation with prototypes, demos, and users in refinement.

**6. "A sponsor wants a feature by a deadline that the team says is impossible."**
> **Clarify:** What's driving the deadline, and what's the minimum needed by then? **Analyse:** Break the feature down; identify the MVP slice. **Act:** Present options: reduced scope by the date (MoSCoW), the full scope later, or added resources with risks. **Confirm:** Agree the plan in writing and track progress transparently.

### Signs of a weak answer

- Jumping straight to "I'd build X"
- Doing everything alone ("I'd just decide")
- Taking sides or blaming people
- No mention of stakeholders, evidence or documentation
- Vague: "I'd communicate with everyone"

## Real-world examples

| Situation in the real job | Good BA response |
|---|---|
| Sales director asked a developer directly for a feature | BA redirected it to the PO, analysed it, and it was prioritised for the next sprint |
| Late regulatory change mid-project | BA ran impact analysis via the traceability matrix; sponsor re-baselined scope |
| Users rejected a new screen at demo | BA ran a quick usability session, redesigned with the team, and avoided a failed launch |

## Step-by-step demonstration

**Question:** *"You've been asked to gather requirements for a new system, but the main stakeholder never has time to meet you. What would you do?"*

**1. Clarify (15 s):** *"First I'd want to understand why: is it workload, low interest, or doubts about the project? And whether there's someone who could act for them."*

**2. Analyse (15 s):** *"I'd look at their power and interest. If they're the decision-maker, their absence is a real risk to the project."*

**3. Act (40 s):** *"I'd make it easy for them: offer short 15-minute slots, send focused questions or options in advance so they only need to decide, and use other sources meanwhile: their team, documents, and data. I'd propose a delegate with authority to make day-to-day decisions. If it still blocks progress, I'd raise the risk with the sponsor or PM, explaining the impact on timeline."*

**4. Confirm (15 s):** *"I'd summarise any decisions in writing for them to confirm, and agree a regular short check-in."*

**5. Optional example (15 s):** *"On a course project, I used a 10-minute weekly decision slot with the sponsor, which kept the project moving."*

Total: about 1.5-2 minutes.

## Practical example

Practise these with Clarify-Analyse-Act-Confirm:

1. "Users are not adopting a system that went live 3 months ago."
2. "You discover a requirement you wrote was wrong after development has started."
3. "The Product Owner wants to skip writing acceptance criteria to save time."
4. "Your data shows a stakeholder's assumption is wrong, and they're very senior."
5. "The development team is in a different time zone and misunderstands stories."

Model outline for #4:
> **Clarify:** check my data and assumptions are sound. **Analyse:** what decision depends on it; impact. **Act:** share privately first, respectfully, focusing on the data not the person; offer options. **Confirm:** agree next steps; document the decision.

## Common mistakes

1. **Answering with a solution immediately.** Start with clarifying questions.
2. **Being the hero.** BAs facilitate; decisions belong to the right roles.
3. **Forgetting to close the loop.** Mention validation and documentation.
4. **Too long.** 1.5-2 minutes is enough; let them probe.
5. **Generic statements.** Name specific techniques: MoSCoW, impact analysis, RACI, prototypes.
